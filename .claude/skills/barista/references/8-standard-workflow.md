# § 8 · Standard Workflow

## Shift Opening Procedures

### Pre-Service Setup (15-20 minutes before opening)

1. **Machine Warm-Up**
   - Turn on espresso machine 15+ minutes before first customer
   - Allow boiler to reach operating temperature (indicator light should stabilize)
   - Run water through group head to flush and warm it
   - Test steam wand with blank (steam into air to remove water)

2. **Grinder Preparation**
   - Purge grinder (run a small amount through without collecting)
   - This removes staleness from previous session
   - Dial in first batch of beans with 1-2 test shots
   - Pull and taste; adjust until balanced (sweet, rich, no sourness)

3. **Milk Station Setup**
   - Ensure milk is properly refrigerated
   - Check expiration dates on all dairy and alternatives
   - Clean pitcher and place at station
   - Verify thermometer is accessible

4. **Workspace Cleanliness**
   - Wipe all surfaces with damp cloth
   - Empty and rinse drip tray
   - Organize cups, lids, sleeves in reach
   - Check equipment for visible damage

5. **Final Checks**
   - Test one full espresso shot (pull, taste, adjust if needed)
   - Verify steam wand functions properly
   - Confirm all tools are within reach
   - Set music/environment to welcoming level

## Standard Service Workflow

### Greeting & Taking Order (5-10 seconds)

1. **Acknowledge customer immediately**
   - Eye contact, smile, warm greeting
   - Use name if regular: "Morning, Sarah! The usual?"

2. **Clarify order if needed**
   - For new customers: ask if they want it for here or to-go
   - Offer seasonal specials without being pushy
   - Note any dietary restrictions or allergies

3. **Confirm & repeat back**
   - Prevents order mistakes
   - Shows you're listening
   - Example: "One double shot cappuccino, 2% milk, cinnamon on top—coming right up!"

---

### Espresso Preparation (40-50 seconds for one drink)

1. **Dose & Level** (5 sec)
   - Measure correct weight into portafilter
   - Level grounds with distribution tool or WDT
   - Tamp with 30lbs perpendicular pressure

2. **Insert & Brew** (5 sec)
   - Insert portafilter firmly into group head
   - Activate pump/lever
   - Watch initial flow (should be slow, golden brown)

3. **Monitor Extraction** (25-30 sec)
   - Total time should be 25-30 seconds
   - Watch pour color (should darken and flow slow)
   - Stop when yield is correct (36g for double)

4. **Quality Check** (5 sec)
   - Assess crema color (golden with tiger stripes is ideal)
   - Note any channeling (uneven flow = poor extraction)
   - Taste if unsure about dialing in

---

### Milk Steaming (30-40 seconds per drink)

1. **Pitcher Prep** (5 sec)
   - Fill pitcher 1/3 to 1/2 with cold milk
   - Purge steam wand briefly before inserting
   - Place pitcher on scale if adjusting portion sizes

2. **Aeration Phase** (5 sec)
   - Insert wand tip 1cm below surface
   - Open steam valve
   - Create light hissing sound (indicates air incorporation)
   - Watch milk rise in pitcher; pull wand out as milk rises

3. **Blending Phase** (15-20 sec)
   - Lower pitcher so wand is barely submerged
   - Increase steam flow
   - Create rolling motion (not sloshing, but smooth circulation)
   - Monitor temperature with thermometer (aim for 60-65°C)

4. **Finish** (3-5 sec)
   - Turn off steam when temperature reaches 65°C
   - Remove wand and immediately clean with damp cloth
   - Tap pitcher on counter to settle large bubbles
   - Swirl gently to integrate foam

---

### Drink Assembly & Pouring

1. **Espresso First**
   - Pour shots directly into cup
   - Wait 1-2 seconds for crema to settle

2. **Pour Milk** (5-15 seconds for latte art)
   - Hold cup steady (slightly tilted if pouring art)
   - Pour from pitcher at consistent height
   - Control flow rate with pitcher angle
   - Watch for desired pattern (rosetta, tulip, or simple heart)

3. **Final Touches**
   - Add toppings if requested (cinnamon, cocoa, etc.)
   - Secure lid for to-go cups
   - Place on cup sleeve for insulation
   - Write name on cup for counter service

## Service During Rush Hour

### Efficiency Prioritization
1. **Pre-pull shots** when you anticipate high volume
2. **Call out times** so customers know wait expectations
3. **Batch milk steaming** (steam for multiple cups in sequence)
4. **Simplified drinks** during peak (prioritize consistency over art)
5. **Communicate** if someone needs to wait: "That'll be 3 minutes—thanks for your patience"

### Stress Management
- Breathing: take 3 deep breaths between customers
- Mistakes happen: own them quickly, don't dwell
- Ask for help if overwhelmed (call another barista)
- Maintain one "quality line" of perfect drinks over rushing through mediocre ones

---

## Closing Procedures (15-20 minutes after last customer)

1. **Machine Shutdown**
   - Stop taking orders (post "last call" 15 min before close)
   - Pull final shots and purge group head
   - Clean group head with backflushing
   - Turn off espresso machine (allow cool-down before cleaning)

2. **Deep Cleaning**
   - Soak portafilter basket and handle in espresso cleaner
   - Clean steam wand thoroughly (inside and outside)
   - Backflush group head multiple times
   - Wipe all espresso machine surfaces

3. **Milk Station**
   - Return milk to refrigerator
   - Clean and store pitcher
   - Wipe down station thoroughly

4. **Workspace**
   - Empty and clean drip tray
   - Wipe all counters and prep surfaces
   - Sweep floor around machine
   - Organize cups and supplies for next shift

5. **Final Walkthrough**
   - Turn off all equipment
   - Lock up cash/register
   - Check that nothing is left on
   - Secure café (lock doors, security if applicable)

## Special Scenarios

### Handling a Complaint

1. **Listen without interrupting** (20-30 seconds)
2. **Acknowledge immediately** ("I understand why you'd be disappointed")
3. **Take responsibility** (avoid blame-shifting)
4. **Fix it** (remake the drink, offer an alternative)
5. **Learn** (note what went wrong for next time)

### Customization Requests

| Request | Response |
|---------|----------|
| "Less foam" | Acknowledge; pour milk with less aeration |
| "Extra hot" | Note 65°C is max before milk scalds; offer explanation if interested |
| "Specific temperature" | "I can aim for 60°C—sound good?" |
| "Unusual milk alternative" | Be honest if you've never used it; offer to try or suggest what you know works |

### Regular Customer Recognition

- Remember and use their name
- Know their usual order
- Greet them warmly even if you're busy
- Personalize: "How was that pour-over last week?" or "Did you like the new Ethiopian?"
- Builds loyalty and makes them feel valued
