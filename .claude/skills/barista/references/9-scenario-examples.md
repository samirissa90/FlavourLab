# § 9 · Scenario Examples

## Scenario 1: First-Time Latte Customer

**Customer:** "I've never had a specialty coffee drink before. What do you recommend?"

**Barista Approach:**
1. **Ask questions** to understand their preference:
   - "Do you usually like sweet drinks or more bitter flavors?"
   - "How do you feel about milk in coffee?"
   - "What sounds better: smooth or creamy?"

2. **Make a recommendation** based on answers:
   - Sweet + milk lover → Latte or cappuccino
   - Less sweet + curious → Flat white or macchiato
   - Black coffee → Single/double espresso or americano

3. **Explain briefly** (not an essay):
   - "A latte has lots of steamed milk and a small dot of foam—really smooth and mellow"
   - "A cappuccino is more espresso-forward with airy foam on top"

4. **Ask follow-up after they drink:**
   - "How was that? Did that work for you?"
   - Use feedback to refine future orders
   - If they loved it: "That's a keeper—your usual next time?"

## Scenario 2: Espresso Machine Broken Mid-Shift

**Situation:** Machine stopped pulling shots. Five customers waiting.

**Barista Workflow:**
1. **Acknowledge immediately** (don't disappear):
   - Appear at counter within 10 seconds
   - "Hey everyone, our espresso machine just went down. I'm going to try a quick fix."

2. **Troubleshoot quickly** (60 seconds max):
   - Check if water is flowing (might be a water line issue)
   - Look for visible error codes or pressure gauge behavior
   - If no obvious fix: call a technician

3. **Communicate with customers:**
   - "It looks like it'll take a few minutes. Can I get you a [alternative drink] while we wait?"
   - Suggest pour-over, Americano (if you have an alternative), tea, etc.
   - Offer discount or free item for inconvenience

4. **Don't panic** (machines break—it happens):
   - Stay calm; it sets the tone
   - Have backup options ready
   - Most customers are understanding if you communicate honestly

## Scenario 3: Regular Customer Ordering a New Drink

**Customer:** "I've been thinking—instead of my usual cappuccino, what if I tried something different?"

**Barista Approach:**
1. **Celebrate the exploration**
   - "Oh, I love that you're open to trying something new!"

2. **Narrow options carefully:**
   - "Since you love the creaminess of a cappuccino, want to try a flat white? It's similar but with velvety microfoam instead of airy foam."
   - Or: "If you want to taste more of the espresso, try a macchiato—just a tiny bit of foam"

3. **Make it happen:**
   - Pull a great shot (you know their taste)
   - Dial in the milk specifically for their preference
   - Pour carefully

4. **Get feedback immediately:**
   - "What do you think? Better, worse, or back to the cappuccino?"
   - Use this feedback to suggest variations next time

5. **Respect their answer:**
   - If they like it: "Great! I'll remember that for next time"
   - If they prefer the original: "That's totally fair—your cappuccino is coming right up tomorrow"

## Scenario 4: Rude or Impatient Customer

**Customer:** "This is taking forever. How long is this coffee supposed to take?"

**Barista Response (keeping calm):**

1. **Validate their frustration** (even if it's unfair):
   - "I know waiting is frustrating. I'm making sure it's perfect for you."
   - Avoid defensiveness or blame

2. **Be transparent:**
   - "Espresso takes about 30 seconds to pull, then another 30-40 seconds to steam milk. This one's almost ready."
   - Or: "I'm pulling a fresh shot because the first one didn't taste right to me. I want you to have a good drink."

3. **Follow through:**
   - Hand them the drink promptly
   - "Here you go—thanks for your patience"

4. **Don't argue** (even if they're wrong):
   - You can't win an argument with a customer; you can only lose
   - Stay professional and kind
   - If they're abusive: "I want to help, but I can only do that if we're respectful. How can I make this right?"

## Scenario 5: Milk Steaming Technique Breakdown

**Problem:** Milk won't froth; it's staying thin and watery.

**Diagnosis & Fix:**

1. **Check milk temperature first:**
   - Thermometer reading: Is it actually 60-65°C?
   - If >70°C, you've over-steamed and milk lost structure
   - Solution: Get fresh milk; start over

2. **Check steam wand positioning:**
   - Wand tip should be 1cm below surface during aeration
   - Too deep = you're just heating, not aerating
   - Solution: Bring wand up during aeration phase

3. **Check milk age/type:**
   - Is milk fresh? Expired milk won't froth
   - Is it a milk alternative that doesn't steam well (almond)?
   - Solution: Try fresh milk or switch to oat/soy which steam more reliably

4. **Check steam pressure:**
   - Is steam actually coming out? (Test with a cup of water)
   - Is the wand clogged with old milk?
   - Solution: Clean steam wand with pipe cleaner; check machine pressure

## Scenario 6: Latte Art Consistency Issues

**Problem:** Some rosetta patterns look perfect; others look like a blob.

**Root Causes & Solutions:**

| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----|
| **Inconsistent pour speed** | Pitcher tilt varies between pours | Use same pitcher angle every time |
| **Uneven milk texture** | Aeration incomplete or too much air | Aerate 3-5 seconds only; blend 15-20 seconds |
| **Cup too full** | No room for latte art | Leave 1cm space at top |
| **Pouring too fast** | Milk hits espresso too hard; foam breaks up | Slow, steady pour; watch the white dot form |
| **Espresso too dark/thin** | Bad extraction; no crema to hold pattern | Dial in first; pull a golden-brown shot |

## Scenario 7: Equipment Maintenance During Service

**Problem:** Espresso machine is running slowly; shots are taking 40+ seconds.

**Quick Diagnosis:**

1. **Check grind:**
   - Is it dialed in? (Grind might be too fine)
   - Solution: Turn grinder 1 click coarser; test

2. **Check tamping:**
   - Are you tamping consistently? (Too much pressure slows flow)
   - Solution: Use 30lbs pressure, not 50+

3. **Check group head:**
   - Is water flowing evenly from all holes?
   - If not: sediment or old oil buildup
   - Solution: Backflush 5-10 times; clean group screen

4. **Check basket:**
   - Is basket old/damaged? (Can cause channeling and slow flow)
   - Solution: Replace basket if >6 months old

## Scenario 8: Customer with Dietary Restriction

**Customer:** "I'm lactose intolerant. What can I drink?"

**Barista Response:**

1. **Confirm the restriction:**
   - "Complete lactose intolerance, or just sensitive?"
   - Some can tolerate a little dairy; others can't

2. **Offer options:**
   - Espresso/Americano (black)
   - Any milk alternative you have (oat, soy, almond)
   - Confirm you've steamed in a clean pitcher (no cross-contamination)

3. **Be honest about alternatives:**
   - "Oat milk steams beautifully and tastes great"
   - "Almond milk is trickier—it can scald. I'll be careful though"

4. **Execute perfectly:**
   - You now have higher responsibility (possible allergic reaction)
   - Use separate equipment if possible
   - Double-check the drink before handing it over
   - "This is steamed in a clean pitcher with oat milk—no dairy"

5. **Build a regular:**
   - If they come back, remember their restriction
   - You've now made them feel safe and welcome
