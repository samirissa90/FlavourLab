# Coffee Origins in Latin America and the Caribbean: Regions, Altitude, Processing, Harvest, Flavor, Production, Grading, Auction Records

Research date: 2026-11-19. Latest USDA FAS circular used: "Coffee: World Markets and Trade", July 2026 (next release 16 Dec 2026). Marketing year (MY) 2025/26 is the latest estimate and 2026/27 is a forecast.

Source-quality legend used below:
- [P] = primary or near-primary document that I fetched and read (USDA FAS circular, Hawaii Dept. of Agriculture list).
- [S] = secondary but fetched and read (importer pages, Perfect Daily Grind, study-guide site, Prensa Libre).
- [Snip] = taken only from a web-search result summary; the page itself was not fetched. Treat as lower confidence.
- I could not retrieve any ANACAFE, ICAFE, IHCAFE, FNC, CONAB, SCAP or ICO document directly. All regional and grading data below is therefore secondary. The ICO historical production file returned 404.

## 1. Countries and named sub-regions: altitude, varieties, processing, harvest, soil, flavor (acidity/body/sweetness 1-10)

### Takeaway
Altitude, variety, harvest and flavor data were found for Colombia, Brazil, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Panama, and Honduras (strongest coverage) and only thinly for El Salvador, Nicaragua, Mexico, Peru, Jamaica and Kona. Nothing usable was found for Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela, Haiti, Dominican Republic, Cuba, Puerto Rico, or the named sub-regions of Salvador/Nicaragua/Peru. Secondary sources conflict on many altitude and harvest values, so every conflict is flagged. No source supplied numeric acidity/body/sweetness scores; all 1-10 values below are my inferences from tasting descriptors.

### Cited Findings

#### Colombia
- Huila 1,500-2,100 masl; red apple, panela, bright orange citrus, yellow peach; medium-to-heavy body — [Open-Exam-Prep SCA study guide, South America](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S]. A different retailer table gives Huila 1,500-2,000 m with dark chocolate, red fruit, caramel — [The Way To Coffee via search](https://www.thewaytocoffee.com/colombian-coffee-regions/) [Snip].
- Nariño 1,700-2,300 masl; crystalline citric/malic acidity, Meyer lemon, apricot, blackcurrant, honeysuckle, very sweet; text cites 18-20 C diurnal swing; same page is internally inconsistent ("farms frequently exceed 2,200 m" vs a quiz saying "exceeding 2,100 m") — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].
- Cauca (Inzá and Popayán) 1,600-2,100 masl; sparkling acidity, red currant, sugarcane, sweet cherry, lemon zest, tea-like — same page [S].
- Tolima 1,500-2,000 masl; stone fruit, tangerine, caramel, juicy mouthfeel — same page [S].
- Antioquia 1,300-1,800 masl; milk chocolate, hazelnut, mild citrus, panela, medium body — same page [S].
- Quindío, Santander, Sierra Nevada: no altitude numbers found. Qualitative only: central belt (Antioquia, Caldas, Quindío) soft nuttiness, big sweetness, low acidity; northern regions (Santander, Santa Marta) lower altitude, full-bodied, less brightness; Nariño/Cauca/Huila more complex acidity and florality — [Cafe Imports Colombia](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/colombia) [S].
- Varieties: Typica, Bourbon, Caturra, Variedad Colombia (1982), Castillo (2005), Cenicafé 1 (2016) — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S]; Cafe Imports also lists Catimor — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/colombia) [S].
- Processing: washed (Colombia is arabica-only and predominantly washed) — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/colombia) [S].
- Harvest (CONFLICTING):
  - Central zone (Antioquia, Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda, Tolima): main Oct-Dec, mitaca Apr-Jun. Southern zone (Huila, Cauca, Nariño): main Apr-Jul, mitaca Oct-Dec. Northern zone: single Oct-Jan crop. Mitaca is 20-40% of annual farm output, about 5-6 months after main crop — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].
  - Contradicted by Cafe Imports: Huila main Nov-Jan, fly May-Jul; Cauca and Nariño main May-Jul, fly Nov-Jan — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/colombia) [S].
  - Another summary says Nariño has one harvest, May-Aug — [search summary](https://www.thewaytocoffee.com/colombian-coffee-regions/) [Snip].
- Grade: Supremo = screen 17/18 (page states over 80% of Castillo beans meet it); Excelso not defined on any fetched page — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].

#### Brazil
- Sul de Minas 850-1,250 masl, about 30% of national output; chocolate, hazelnut, yellow-fruit sweetness — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S]; contradicted by a roaster blog claiming it produces over 50% of Brazil's coffee and another giving 1,000 m average — [Five Senses](https://fivesenses.com.au/blog/part-ii-brazil-one-country-many-flavours) [Snip].
- Cerrado Mineiro 800-1,300 masl, fully mechanized, dry-season harvest May-Sep, first Brazilian certified Denomination of Origin — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S]; one blog says 1,400+ m, a conflict — [Ineffable Coffee](https://ineffablecoffee.com/en/blogs/origin-monographics/cafe-de-brasil) [Snip].
- Mogiana (Sao Paulo) 900-1,100 masl; historic Bourbon estates; creamy, heavy body, toffee — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S]. Another source: mild acidity, medium body, caramel and red fruit — [search summary](https://medium.com/ally-coffee/coffee-regions-of-brazil-a44d31cbdadb) [Snip].
- Matas de Minas 600-1,200 masl; steep terrain, manual harvest; pulped natural and washed micro-lots — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].
- Espírito Santo: epicenter of conilon (robusta); approximately 70% of Brazilian robusta is grown there; over 70% of Brazilian arabica is grown in Minas Gerais — [USDA FAS July 2026](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/circulars/2026/07/coffee.pdf) [P]. No altitude given.
- Chapada Diamantina (Bahia) and Alta Mogiana: no data found. Search results for "Chapada" returned Chapada de Minas (a different region) — [Five Senses](https://fivesenses.com.au/blog/part-ii-brazil-one-country-many-flavours) [Snip].
- Varieties: Yellow Bourbon, Mundo Novo, Catuaí (about 50% of arabica canopy) — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].
- Processing: natural (historical); pulped natural / Cereja Descascada (developed early 1990s; mucilage dries onto parchment over 7-12 days to 11% moisture) — same page [S].
- Cup: low-to-medium soft acidity, milk chocolate, toffee, hazelnut, almond, dense creamy body — same page [S].
- Harvest: Cerrado May-Sep (above). Sul de Minas harvest is reported as both Apr-Sep and Oct-Mar by different blogs — [Five Senses](https://fivesenses.com.au/blog/part-ii-brazil-one-country-many-flavours) [Snip]. USDA marketing year is July-June — [USDA FAS July 2026](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/circulars/2026/07/coffee.pdf) [P].
- Biennial cycle: arabica can swing 5-10 million bags year to year; USDA expects a 2026/27 rebound — [USDA FAS July 2026](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/circulars/2026/07/coffee.pdf) [P].

#### Guatemala (ANACAFE's eight regions)
- Antigua 1,500-1,700 masl; volcanic pumice soil (Fuego ash); milk chocolate, sweet orange, delicate floral, crisp acidity, velvety body — [Open-Exam-Prep, Central America](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. Cafe Imports gives 1,300-1,600 m, sunnier, less rain, "sweet, smooth" (conflict) — [Cafe Imports Guatemala](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala) [S]. Prensa Libre: Antigua "particularly sweet", pumice soil retains moisture — [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732) [S].
- Huehuetenango 1,600-2,200 masl; limestone/metamorphic non-volcanic soil; frost protected by warm dry Tehuantepec winds; crystalline malic/citric acidity, green apple, white peach, apricot, honeysuckle, winey — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. Cafe Imports: "up to 2,000 m", full body, toffee sweetness, fruit-forward — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala) [S].
- Cobán (Alta Verapaz) 1,300-1,500 masl; limestone karst cloud forest; syrupy body, spiced stone fruit, tobacco, cedar — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. Prensa Libre: cloudy rainy climate, fine body — [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732) [S].
- Acatenango Valley: about 2,000 m ("unos dos mil metros") — [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732) [S]. No flavor given in that source.
- Atitlán (Traditional): volcanic slopes, pronounced citric acidity (Prensa Libre); very rich volcanic soil, windy and wet, nutty, chocolate, lemony acidity, florals (Cafe Imports). No altitude in either — [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732), [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala) [S].
- San Marcos: warmest region, rains arrive earlier, floral notes, pronounced acidity, good body — [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732) [S]. No altitude found.
- Nuevo Oriente: cloudier, rainier, stable temperatures; full-bodied, balanced (Cafe Imports); chocolatey (Prensa Libre) — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala), [Prensa Libre](https://www.prensalibre.com/?p=257732) [S].
- Fraijanes Plateau: no altitude, soil, or flavor data found in fetched sources.
- Varieties: Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai, Typica, Maragogype, Pache. Processing: washed or mechanically demucilaged. Harvest Sep-Apr — [Cafe Imports Guatemala](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala) [S]. Bags are 69 kg in Guatemala per the same page.

#### Costa Rica
- Tarrazú 1,200-1,900 masl; grapefruit, green apple, cane sugar, citric brightness — [Open-Exam-Prep, Central America](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. One unattributed table in a search-result set gives 1,200-1,700 m, harvest Dec-Mar (exact page not isolated; result set included [Coffee Review](https://www.coffeereview.com/costa-rica-coffee-2025/)) [Snip].
- West Valley (Naranjo, Palmares, San Ramón) 1,000-1,600 masl; stone fruit, orange blossom, toffee — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. High share of Cup of Excellence winners; Villa Sarchi, Villa Lobos, SL-28, Gesha grown — [Cafe Imports Costa Rica](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/costa-rica) [S].
- Tres Ríos: toffee sweetness, soft citrus, milder; Central Valley: distinct wet/dry seasons, some of the best naturals — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/costa-rica) [S]. Altitude conflict in snippets: 1,200-1,650 vs 1,300-1,800 m — [Cafe Britt regions](https://www.cafebritt.com/blogs/journey-to-origin/8-costa-rican-coffee-regions) [Snip].
- Brunca: 600-1,900 m in one importer listing — [Coffee Hunter](https://coffeehunter.com/our-origins/costa-rica/) [Snip]. No verified flavor data.
- Varieties: Caturra, Catuai, Bourbon, Villa Sarchi, Villa Lobos, SL-28, Gesha, Venesia. Processing: washed, natural, honey (yellow/red/black). Harvest Dec-Apr — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/costa-rica) [S].
- Honey spectrum: white 10-20% mucilage, 6-8 days drying; yellow about 50%, 8-10 days; red 75-80%, 12-15 days; black about 100% (skin removed only), 20-25 days. Flavors: white tea-like/raw sugarcane; yellow apricot/cantaloupe; red raspberry/plum; black dried fig/dark grape/cocoa nibs — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S].
- Robusta was banned about 30 years; ban lifted 2018 — same page [S].
- 47,137 coffee-growing families (2017); nearly 70% of coffee from producers with under 200 exportable bags; under 1% of world production; 69 kg bags — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/costa-rica) [S].

#### Panama
- Boquete 1,200-1,900 masl (eastern, windward; "bajareque" mist) and Volcán/Candela (western, drier, sunnier, warmer; anaerobic lots mentioned) — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. Cafe Imports gives a single 1,000-1,650 m range for all areas (conflict) and notes Volcán has a lot of volcanic soil — [Cafe Imports Panama](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/panama) [S].
- Renacimiento: remote, limited infrastructure; no verified altitude — [Cafe Imports](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/panama) [S].
- Gesha profile: jasmine, bergamot, white peach, kaffir lime, lemongrass, orange blossom, Earl Grey, champagne-like acidity, silky mouthfeel — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S].
- Varieties: Catuai, Caturra, Geisha, Mundo Novo (Bourbon on some farms). Processing: washed and natural. Harvest Nov-Mar. Exports 50,000-100,000 bags a year (60 kg) — [Cafe Imports Panama](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/panama) [S].
- Gesha history: collected in Ethiopia in the 1930s, reached CATIE (Costa Rica) as T2722 in 1953, distributed in Panama in the 1960s, gained attention 2004 via Hacienda La Esmeralda — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S].
- Panama has no formal altitude grade such as SHB in the fetched sources (same pages).

#### Honduras (six IHCAFE-style regions, from Perfect Daily Grind)
All from [Perfect Daily Grind, 6 coffee regions of Honduras](https://perfectdailygrind.com/2017/08/introducing-the-6-coffee-regions-of-honduras/) [S]:
- Copán 1,000-1,500 masl; Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai; chocolate, caramel, citrus, creamy body; coolest low 11.5 C.
- Opalaca 1,100-1,500 masl; Bourbon, Catuai, Typica; tropical fruit, grape, berry, mango, floral.
- Montecillos 1,200-1,600 masl (Marcala sits here; cold nights); citrus, peach, apricot, caramel, velvety body, tartaric acidity.
- Comayagua 1,000-1,500 masl; Typica, Bourbon, Parchi; sweet citrus, bright acidity, creamy rich body.
- El Paraíso 1,000-1,400 masl; Parainema (Sarchimor hybrid) featured; temperature 16-22.5 C; champagne, grape, mango, floral.
- Agalta 1,100-1,400 masl; Bourbon, Caturra, Typica; tropical fruit, caramel, chocolate, sweet finish.
- Processing: washed most common; some honey and natural experimentation. Harvest months: not stated.
- Montecillos is the first Honduran denomination of origin ("Café de Marcala") — [Algrano Honduras via search](https://www.algrano.com/origins/honduras) [Snip].

#### El Salvador, Nicaragua, Mexico, Peru, Caribbean, Hawaii (thin)
- El Salvador: Apaneca-Ilamatepec range 500-2,300 m, most productive region — [Ozone Coffee via search](https://ozonecoffee.co.uk/blogs/journal/coffee-production-in-el-salvador) [Snip]. Pacamara = Pacas x Maragogype, origin date given variously as 1954, 1958 or 1950s-60s — [search summary, multiple sources](https://espressocoffeeguide.com/gourmet-coffee/coffees-of-the-americas/el-salvador-coffee) [Snip]. Chalatenango and Cuscatlán: no data.
- Nicaragua: national altitude 1,100-1,600 m or 1,000-1,700 m depending on source; graded HG/SHG — [Cafe Imports Nicaragua via search](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/nicaragua) [Snip]. Jinotega, Matagalpa, Nueva Segovia, Dipilto: no data found.
- Mexico: Chiapas is the largest producer (Sierra Madre, Tacaná volcano on the Guatemala border); lower-altitude Oaxaca is subtler, less acidic, citrus-forward; Veracruz no details; harvest Nov-Mar — [Cafe Imports Mexico via search](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/mexico), [Mental Floss via search](https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/coffee/touring-24-top-coffee-production-regions) [Snip]. Puebla: no data.
- Peru: Cajamarca and San Martín suit washed processing; Cajamarca's key risk is heavy harvest rain; San Ignacio/Jaén/Chirinos (Cajamarca) cited for high-quality lots; Cusco described both as high-altitude/low-volume and as a natural-process area (conflict) — [Sustainable Harvest via search](https://www.sustainableharvest.com/blog/origin-highlight-exploring-peruvian-coffee) [Snip]. No verified altitude or harvest months.
- Jamaica Blue Mountain: altitude cap 1,700 m per a Wikipedia mirror, 1,800 m per an importer page (conflict); Grade 1 = screen 17+, "Select" = mix of Nos. 1-3 with max 4% minor defects; JACRA inspects every barrel; harvest reported as Aug-Sep, Sep-Apr, or Aug with April and November peaks (conflict, partly marketing pages) — [Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Jamaican_Blue_Mountain_Coffee), [importer specs page](https://frazer-goddon-894w.squarespace.com/en/specifications-quality) [Snip].
- Hawaii Kona: Hawaii Dept. of Agriculture green-coffee grades include Extra Fancy, Fancy, Hawaii No.1, Select, Prime, Hawaii No.3, Natural Prime — [HDOA Grading Standards, Jan 2026](https://dab.hawaii.gov/add/files/2026/02/Grading-Standards-for-Hawaii-Green-Coffee-2023-2025_Jan.2026-final.pdf) [P, per search listing; I did not open the PDF]. Extra Fancy defined by 19/64-inch screen per a roaster — [Paradise Roasters](https://paradiseroasters.com/products/kona-extra-fancy-coffee) [Snip]. Kona altitude and harvest could not be verified (one listing says 150-900 m, Aug-Jan, but the page was unclear).
- Ecuador, Bolivia (Caranavi), Venezuela, Haiti, Dominican Republic, Cuba, Puerto Rico: no region data found.

## 3. Altitude-quality rules and the altitude-density-acidity relationship

### Takeaway
Latin American altitude grades are density-based labels, not cup scores, and thresholds differ (and are reported inconsistently) by country. Guatemala SHB >1,350 m is the most commonly cited value; Costa Rica SHB is cited as >1,200 m in one source and >1,350 m in another; El Salvador SHG is about 1,200 m. Peer-reviewed evidence links higher elevation to denser, more chemically rich beans; the acidity link rests mainly on trade sources.

### Cited Findings
- Guatemala: SHB >1,350 masl; HB 1,200-1,350; SH (Semi-Hard) 1,050-1,200; Prime/Extra Prime 900-1,050 — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]. Variant: SHB above 1,370 m; HB 1,066-1,370 m; Extra Prime 762-1,066 m — [search summary of Guatemala sources](https://www.atlascoffee.com/coffee/guatemala/) [Snip]. Conflicting: Cafe Imports lists SHB 1,600-1,700, FHB 1,500-1,600, HB 1,350-1,500 — [Cafe Imports Guatemala](https://www.cafeimports.com/north-america/guatemala) [S].
- Costa Rica: SHB >1,200 m per Open-Exam-Prep ([source](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/central-american-origins) [S]); contradicted by Cafe Imports: SHB >1,350 m, GHB 1,000-1,350 m ([source](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/costa-rica) [S]). ICAFE regulation not retrieved.
- Honduras: SHG above about 1,200 m and HG 700-1,200 m (most common figure); one source gives SHG 1,350+ and HG 1,200-1,350 — [search summary, Honduras sources](https://www.algrano.com/origins/honduras) [Snip]. SHG is an altitude/density grade, not a quality score (same summary).
- El Salvador: SHG ≥1,200 m (above 3,950 ft), High Grown 900-1,200 m, Central Standard below 900 m — [George Howell Coffee, El Salvador](https://georgehowellcoffee.com/pages/country-el-salvador) [Snip]. One source claims SHB above 1,400 m for El Salvador, which conflicts and is treated as unreliable — [search summary](https://espressocoffeeguide.com/gourmet-coffee/coffees-of-the-americas/el-salvador-coffee) [Snip].
- Nicaragua: graded HG/SHG by altitude; no SHB; cutoffs not found — [search summary](https://interamericancoffee.com/?p=13449) [Snip].
- Colombia: Supremo = screen 17/18; size-based (not altitude-based) — [Open-Exam-Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/sca-q-grader/coffee-geography-terroir-origins/south-american-origins) [S].
- Jamaica: Blue Mountain No.1 = screen 17+; size and defect based — [importer specs](https://frazer-goddon-894w.squarespace.com/en/specifications-quality) [Snip].
- Kona: grades Extra Fancy through Natural Prime, based on shape/size, color, moisture, defects (not altitude) — [HDOA](https://dab.hawaii.gov/add/files/2026/02/Grading-Standards-for-Hawaii-Green-Coffee-2023-2025_Jan.2026-final.pdf) [P per search listing].
- Altitude and chemistry: a 2024 Beverage Plant Research study of Ethiopian arabica from 20 sites found hundred-bean weight, moisture, crude fat, trigonelline and sucrose all rose with elevation, attributed to slower fruit maturation in cooler conditions (DOI 10.48130/bpr-0024-0021) — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1094830) [S].
- Density: a Roast Magazine piece reports a significant correlation between elevation and bean density — [Roast Magazine](https://www.roastmagazine.com/stories/beyond-elevation) [Snip]. A maturation study reportedly found 650 to 935 m extended fruit maturation by four weeks with higher density at 935 m (original page not isolated) — [search results](https://doaj.org/article/a35bde0091d543079094177a4445a764) [Snip].
- Acidity: described as higher at altitude mainly in roaster blogs (general consensus, not measured) — [Simon & Bearns](https://simonandbearns.coffee/en/blogs/kaffeeblog/coffee-terroir-how-altitude-and-climate-shape-flavor) [Snip].

### Inferences
- Because altitude grades are density proxies applied at export, the dataset should store `grade_system`, `grade_name`, `altitude_min_masl`, and a `threshold_confidence` field, and keep conflicting thresholds as separate rows with a source each rather than picking one.
- Costa Rica SHB threshold should be flagged "disputed (1,200 vs 1,350 m)" until ICAFE is checked; Guatemala SHB >1,350 m is the best-supported value but Cafe Imports' 1,600+ is a notable outlier.

### Gaps
- No ANACAFE, ICAFE, IHCAFE or FNC regulation text retrieved. Mexican, Peruvian, Ecuadorian, Bolivian and Panamanian grade systems were not found.
- Excelso screen size (and Supremo versus other screen lists) not sourced.
- Direct measurement linking altitude to acidity for Latin American coffees not found.

## 4. Cup of Excellence and Best of Panama auction records

### Takeaway
The all-time auction record found is the 2025 Best of Panama washed Gesha from Hacienda La Esmeralda at US$30,204/kg (about US$13,700/lb computed). Cup of Excellence records are far lower (Latin American highs in the US$100-300/lb range); the overall COE record found is a non-Latin-American lot at US$445/lb.

### Cited Findings
- Best of Panama 2025: Hacienda La Esmeralda, washed Geisha, lot GW-01, 98.00 points (highest ever at Best of Panama; 22 international judges, cupped at the Boquete Coffee Festival in June), about 20 kg, US$604,080 total = US$30,204/kg; auction run by M-Cultivo, held shortly before 8 Aug 2025 — [Magazine.coffee](https://magazine.coffee/blog/1/6985/new-record-breaking-high-price-paid-at-best-of-panama-auction-2025) [S].
