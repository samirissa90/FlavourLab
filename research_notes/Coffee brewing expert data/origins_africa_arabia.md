## Q1. Growing regions: altitude, varieties, processing, harvest, grades, soil, flavor (by country and sub-region)

### Takeaway
Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda and Tanzania have reasonably consistent descriptions of varieties, processing and harvest windows. Altitude figures vary widely between sources (especially Yemen, Uganda, Ethiopia sub-regions), so each range below is attributed. Sub-region-level data for Guji, Gedeb, Bensa, Nano Challa, Limu, Embu, Thika, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, and Madagascar is thin or missing.

### Cited Findings

#### Ethiopia
- Production-side facts: ~130+ widely cultivated regional or local landraces; Jimma Agricultural Research Centre (JARC) varieties; most washing stations run both fully washed and natural; smallholders deliver cherry to cooperative or private wet mills; some process natural at home on raised beds — [Sucafina Ethiopia](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/ethiopia)
- Harvest months: low elevations October-December; high elevations November-January — [Sucafina Ethiopia](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/ethiopia)
- Regions Sucafina names: Djimma, Limu, Nekempt, Guji, Yirgacheffe, Gedeb, Sidamo, Kochere — [Sucafina Ethiopia](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/ethiopia)
- Sidama (Sidamo): 1,500 to 2,200 masl, "Strictly High Grown"; flavor "lemon and citrus with bright crisp acidity" — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Kaffa: 1,400 to 2,100 masl; grows wild or semi-forest, "not cultivated nor maintained" — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Harar (Harrar): eastern highlands, dry (natural) processed, hand sorted; flavor "fruity, wine flavour," medium acidity, full body, "distinctive mocha flavour"; Mocha Harar peaberry "complex chocolate, spice and citrus" — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Genika (Bench Maji zone): "deep, spice and wine or chocolate-like taste and floral aroma" — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Bean-size classes Longberry / Shortberry / Mocha are described by Wikipedia (not a G1-G5 system) — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Sidamo, Harar and Yirgacheffe are trademarked/licensed regional names owned by Ethiopia; Starbucks agreement signed 20 June 2007 — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia)
- Guji was formerly part of Sidama, distinct since 2002; Yirgacheffe is a subregion of Sidama; Yirgacheffe typically washed; Guji traditionally washed and natural, some honey now; Limu = parts of Jimma zone processed wet (search-synthesis, page-level mapping not verified) — [Coffee Hunter Ethiopia](https://coffeehunter.com/our-origins/ethiopia/) and [Specialty Kava Ethiopia](https://specialtykava.si/en/coffee-origins-ethiopia/) **[unverified page-level]**
- Altitude claims from search snippets (units as given; not tied to a specific page): Yirgacheffe 5,500-7,200 ft; Harrar 4,600-6,500 ft; Guji 6,200-6,500 ft; one Limu cooperative 1,975-2,200 m — candidate sources [Coffee Hunter](https://coffeehunter.com/our-origins/ethiopia/), [Specialty Kava](https://specialtykava.si/en/coffee-origins-ethiopia/), [cworks](https://www.cworks.co.uk/blogs/blog/what-is-the-best-coffee-from-ethiopia) **[unverified page-level]**
- Washed Yirgacheffe descriptors: jasmine, bergamot, honeysuckle, lemon, lime, black tea; natural lots: blueberry, strawberry, peach jam, milk chocolate — [Copenhagen Coffee Lab on Yirgacheffe](https://copenhagencoffeelab.com/en/blogs/inside-the-lab/yirgacheffe-kaffe-den-etiopiske-kaffe-alle-taler-om), [Gevi: Washed vs Natural Yirgacheffe](https://gevi.com/blogs/coffee-knowledge/yirgacheffe-washed-vs-natural-processing-flavor), [Coffee Review natural Yirgacheffe Wote Konga](https://www.coffeereview.com/review/ethiopia-natural-yirgacheffe-wote-konga/) (roaster/retail notes, not controlled cupping data)
- Ethiopian Commodity Exchange (ECX) established 2008; ~88% of exported coffee bought via ECX as of 2017; 2017 reforms allowed traceable direct sales — [Sucafina Ethiopia](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/ethiopia)
- Ethiopian production increase attributed to replacing over half of cultivated area with higher-yielding varieties over the last 4 years — [USDA FAS July 2026](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/circulars/2026/07/coffee.pdf)

#### Kenya
- Regions (Sucafina): Central (Nyeri, Muranga, Kirinyaga, Kiambu); Eastern (Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Embu, Machakos); Coast (Taita Taveta); Western (Bungoma, Kakamega); Rift Valley (Nakuru, Kericho, Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu, Baringo); Nyanza (Kisii, Nyamira) — [Sucafina Kenya](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/kenya)
- Varieties: SL28, SL34, K7, Ruiru 11, Batian, Kent; most lots are blends because farms are too small to separate by variety; mostly fully washed, dried on raised beds — [Sucafina Kenya](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/kenya)
- SL28 and SL34 selected by Scott Laboratories 1935-1939 (drought resistance); Batian released 2010, a single-tree selection from fifth-generation Ruiru 11 stock — search synthesis of [Royal Coffee Kenya AA FAQ](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=826191) and importer pages **[unverified page-level]**
- Harvest for Central Kenya: May-July (early crop) and October-December (late crop) — [Sucafina Kenya](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/kenya)
- Grading is by bean size: AA = screen 17/18, AB = screen 15/16, PB = peaberry; grade is a size measure, not defect tolerance, and AB can out-cup AA — [Royal Coffee Kenya AB FAQ](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=825659), [Royal Coffee Kenya AA FAQ](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=826191) (snippet synthesis, **[unverified page-level]**)
- Altitudes: Royal lists 1,600-2,200 masl for an AA lot and 1,400-1,800 masl for an AB lot; general Kenya range 1,400-2,200 m — [Royal Coffee Kenya AA FAQ](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=826191), [Royal Coffee Kenya AB FAQ](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=825659) **[unverified page-level]**
- Central region (Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang'a) ~60% of the crop — [Royal NY](https://www.royalny.com/?p=41846) **[unverified page-level]**
- Soil: "acidic soil in the highlands of central Kenya"; classified "Colombia Mild"; descriptors "intense flavor, full body... notes of cocoa" (generic) — [Wikipedia: Kenyan coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_coffee). Sucafina says roasters blend Kenyan "bright character" with chocolatey South American coffees — [Sucafina Kenya](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/kenya)
- Auction: weekly auction central since before independence (government-run auction in place by 1934); mandatory before 2006; 2006 law created a "second window" for direct sales; 2018 law added further options — [Sucafina Kenya](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/kenya). Nairobi Coffee Exchange sold $127.8 million in 2022-23, down 43.8% from $227.3 million in 2021-22 — [Wikipedia: Kenyan coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_coffee)
- Rainfall/temperature figures for Kenya: none found (see Gaps).

#### Rwanda
- Red Bourbon, French Mission Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai, Mibirizi; Bourbon ~95% of trees; 400,000+ smallholders mostly under 0.25 ha; harvest March-June; 300+ Central Washing Stations (first 2001); improved processing added at least a 40% premium — [Sucafina Rwanda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/rwanda)
- Provinces: West (along Lake Kivu), South, North, East — [Sucafina Rwanda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/rwanda)
- National altitude 1,400-2,000 m or 1,500-2,000 m; lot-level: Nyamasheke lots ~1,750 m and 1,800-2,200 m, a Huye lot ~2,000 m — [Cup of Excellence farm directory entry](https://allianceforcoffeeexcellence.org/farm-directory/86-62-7), [Cafe Imports Rwanda blog](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/blog/?p=60653) **[unverified page-level; lot-level only]**
- Nyamasheke: lake-moderated climate; Gakenke: higher, cooler, "sparkling acidity"; harvest sometimes to August in Gakenke — [Cafe Imports Rwanda blog](https://www.cafeimports.com/europe/blog/?p=60653) **[unverified page-level]**
- Sucafina offers Gakenke natural and fully washed lots, confirming natural processing in Gakenke — [Sucafina Gakenke natural](https://sucafina.com/na/offerings/gakenke-natural-impact)

#### Burundi
- Kayanza and Ngozi are the key provinces; varieties Bourbon, French Mission Bourbon, Jackson, Mibirizi; harvest March-July; ~600,000 smallholders; flavor "super sweet, clean and often floral" — [Sucafina Burundi](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/burundi)
- Station altitudes: Kibingo 1,893 masl — [Sucafina Kibingo FW](https://sucafina.com/emea/offerings/kibingo-fw-impact-beyond-flagship); Nkanda 1,600-2,000 masl — [Coffee Hunter Nkanda](https://coffeehunter.com/coffee/nkanda-1/)
- Regional averages Kayanza ~1,700 m, Ngozi ~1,650 m; Ngozi holds 25% of washing stations — [Helena Coffee Burundi overview](https://www.helenacoffee.vn/?p=21352) **[unverified page-level]**
- Sucafina offers fully washed, honey (Yandaro) and natural (Gahahe, Masha, Kibingo) lots — [Sucafina Yandaro honey](https://sucafina.com/na/offerings/yandaro-honey-impact-beyond-flagship), [Sucafina Gahahe natural](https://sucafina.com/emea/offerings/gahahe-natural-impact-beyond-farmgate)

#### Uganda
- Arabica varieties Kent, Typica, SL14, SL28; DRUGAR (dried unsorted cherry, ground-dried) and WUGAR (semi-washed, or washed Western Arabica); washed lots mostly hand-pulped — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda)
- Regions: Bugisu (Mt Elgon, volcanic soil), Western (Rwenzori), Lake Victoria basin, West Nile — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda)
- Arabica harvest: January-April (main south, fly north) and September-October (fly south, main north) — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda). Mt Elgon most often given October-February (conflicting: Starbucks sheet gives September-February) — [Royal Coffee](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=829871) **[unverified page-level]**, [Starbucks Uganda sheet](https://live-sgacms.ws.asu.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/Uganda_ENG_0.pdf)
- Mt Elgon altitudes vary by source: 1,200-2,200 m ([Counter Culture Mountain Harvest](https://counterculturecoffee.com/products/mountain-harvest)); 1,300-2,200 m ([Espresso Coffee Guide](https://espressocoffeeguide.com/gourmet-coffee/arabian-and-african-coffees/uganda-coffee)); 1,300-2,600 m ([The Way to Coffee](https://www.thewaytocoffee.com/beans/bugisu-aa/)); 1,500-2,500 m ([Royal NY](https://www.royalny.com/?p=72671)). Rwenzori (Mubuku, Kasese): washing station ~1,020 m, farms ~1,300-2,030 m — [Sucafina Mubuku](https://www.sucafina.com/na/offerings/mubuku-kasese-fw)
- Bugisu ("Nyasaland") variety grown almost only on Mt Elgon, arrived 1910; Mt Elgon is the washed zone, Rwenzori the natural zone — [Royal Coffee](https://royalcoffee.com/?p=829871), [Atlas Coffee Uganda](https://www.atlascoffee.com/coffee/uganda/) **[unverified page-level]**
- Bugisu flavor: "winey, fruity, traditionally East African cupping notes" — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda)
- Robusta harvest May-August (southwest/west) and November-January — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda)

#### Tanzania
- Northern: Kilimanjaro, Arusha, Karatu/Ngorongoro, Tarime, Kigoma, Kagera; Southern: Ruvuma, Mbeya/Mbozi; southern regions now ~75-85% of production; smallholders >90%; varieties Arusha, Bourbon, Blue Mountain, Kent; fully washed (water intensive), natural being developed in two southern co-ops; auction since 1980s similar to Nairobi (Moshi) plus direct sales — [Sucafina Tanzania](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/tanzania)
- Harvest: Sucafina is internally inconsistent (May-November in details; July-December in text) — [Sucafina Tanzania](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/tanzania). Starbucks sheet: May-September north, July-September south — [Starbucks Tanzania map](https://live-sgacms.ws.asu.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/Tanzania%20Map_Updated.pdf) (via search snippet) **[unverified page-level]**
- Altitudes: general arabica band 1,000-2,500 m (Starbucks sheet); Kilimanjaro 1,050-2,500 m; Southern Highlands 1,200-1,800 m — [Starbucks Tanzania map](https://live-sgacms.ws.asu.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/Tanzania%20Map_Updated.pdf), [Coffeeness Tanzania](https://www.coffeeness.de/en/tanzania-coffee/) **[unverified page-level]**
- N39 is a TACRI-developed Bourbon-type cultivar; Kent no longer considered rust resistant; K7 tolerant — [HomeGrounds N39](https://www.homegrounds.co/n39-coffee/), [Royal Coffee Tanzania review](https://royalcoffee.com/tanzania-export-review/) **[unverified page-level]**

#### Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe
- Malawi: regions Chitipa, Rumphi, Viphya, Nkhata Bay Highlands, South East Mzimba, Ntchisi East; co-ops Misuku Hills, Phoka Hills, Viphya North, Nkhatabay Highlands, SE Mzimba, Ntchisi East; varieties Agaro, Geisha, Catimor, Mundo Novo, Caturra; harvest April-October; Sable Farms produces washed Arabica — [Sucafina Malawi](https://sucafina.com/na/origins/malawi)
- Malawi altitudes (importer-level): Misuku Hills 1,700-2,000 m; Nkhata Bay Highlands 1,000-2,000 m — [Interamerican Malawi Mzuzu AB](https://www.interamericancoffee.com/malawi-mzuzu-ab/) **[unverified page-level]**
- Zambia: Muchinga and Lusaka areas, 900-2,000 m, harvest April-September, SL34 and Catimor traditional, newer Starmaya and Marsellesa — [Algrano Zambia](https://algrano.com/origins/zambia) **[unverified page-level]**
- Zimbabwe: Chimanimani (near Mozambique border) mentioned; exports re-emerging in specialty — [GCR: Coffee's growth in southern Africa](https://www.gcrmag.com/coffees-growth-in-southern-africa) **[unverified page-level]**. No altitude/variety/harvest data found.

#### DRC, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Madagascar
- DRC Kivu (North Kivu, South Kivu, Maniema): Arabica 1,460-2,000 m (4,800-6,560 ft), robusta in lowlands; arabica about one fifth of total; 2006 output 100,000 bags arabica and 470,000 bags robusta; national output fell from 120,000 t (1993) to 8,000 t (2016) — [Helena Coffee DRC](https://www.helenacoffee.vn/?p=21385), [Global Press Journal](https://globalpressjournal.com/africa/democratic-republic-of-congo/drcs-dominant-coffee-industry-struggles-survive/) **[unverified page-level; dated]**
- Cameroon: seven regions (West, Northwest, Littoral, Southwest, South, Centre, East); arabica on the Bamileke and Bamoun high plateaus, robusta mid-elevation; no meter values — [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Cameroon)
- Ivory Coast: robusta producer (USDA robusta table); Madagascar: mainly robusta, introduced from Réunion — [USDA FAS July 2026](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/circulars/2026/07/coffee.pdf), [Wikipedia: Coffee production in Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Madagascar). No altitude, region or harvest data found for either.

#### Yemen
- Five main regions per one trade guide: Bani Matar, Bani Hammad, Bura'a, Haraz, Haimateen; Haraz regarded as the finest — [Ozone Coffee Yemen](https://ozonecoffee.co.uk/blogs/journal/new-release-yemen-ali-kabeer-jaadi-natural)
- Landrace varieties Udaini, Dawairi, Tuffahi, Jaadi (often grouped "Yemenia") — [Coffee Origins Yemen/Haraz](https://coffee-origins.pages.dev/origins/yemen-haraz) **[low reliability]**
- Altitude is contested: Haraz 1,800-2,400 m vs "at least 3,000 masl"; Bani Matar 1,800-2,200 m vs 2,500-3,000 m; broader Yemen overview 1,200-2,400 m — [Coffee Origins Yemen/Haraz](https://coffee-origins.pages.dev/origins/yemen-haraz), [Contour Coffee Yemen](https://contour-coffee.pages.dev/origins/yemen) **[low reliability]**
- Harvest October-February, hand-picked in several passes; sun-dried natural on raised beds — [Coffee Origins Yemen/Haraz](https://coffee-origins.pages.dev/origins/yemen-haraz) **[low reliability, single source]**
- Historical processing "very rudimentary"; Mocha was for centuries the only export gateway; Dutch smuggled seedstock to Indonesia; Mocha and Java blended by the late 1800s — [CoffeeGeek: Yemen](https://www.coffeegeek.com/history/yemen-the-ancient-origins-of-coffee)
- Mocha today: old port in ruins; most exports leave from Al-Hudayda — [Mocha-Java overview (search synthesis)](https://www.homegrounds.co/why-is-coffee-called-java/) **[unverified page-level]**
- Flavor: Yemen Mocha dry-processed, powerful fruity acidity, "high, winy, often rough," more body and richness than Harrar; "Red-Sea wildness" = slightly rotten edge to fruitiness; Bani Matar "balanced acidity and smooth body" — [Coffee Review: Mocha-Java blends](https://www.coffeereview.com/mocha-java-blends/), [Ozone Coffee Yemen](https://ozonecoffee.co.uk/blogs/journal/new-release-yemen-ali-kabeer-jaadi-natural)
- Hodeidah and Sana'a as named sub-regions: Sana'a appears only as the reference city for Haraz (west) and Bani Matar (west); no Hodeidah growing-area data found.

#### Saudi Arabia (Jazan, Khawlani)
- Jazan coffee grown 800-2,000 masl; harvest October-January; Al-Dayer governorate ~80% of Jazan production, 994 farms and >218,000 trees — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/1618606)
- Khawlani: Coffea arabica, harvest October-January, ripe fruit dried in shade and stirred for 3 weeks; Jazan governorates Al-Dayer (65% of mountain farmers), Fayfa (12%), Al-Reeth, Al-Edabi, Al-Aridhah, Harub; UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription 30 Nov 2022 ("Knowledge and practices related to cultivating Khawlani coffee beans") — [Wikipedia: Khawlani Coffee Beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khawlani_Coffee_Beans), [KSA for UNESCO](https://ksaforunesco.org/?p=5502)
- No named cultivar and no tasting notes in the fetched sources.

#### Mocha-Java context
- Considered the oldest recorded blend; Yemen's "lively, fruity acidity" brightens low-toned Java; many blenders substitute Ethiopia Harrar (also dry-processed, similar winy acidity, less body) for Yemen, and Sumatra for estate Java; the author's cupping of ten blends used six Yemen, three Harrar, one Yirgacheffe — [Coffee Review: Mocha-Java blends](https://www.coffeereview.com/mocha-java-blends/)
- Name not protected, so not a fixed recipe; one-third Mocha to two-thirds Java ratio preferred by end of 19th century (encyclopedia entry via search, origin date unknown) — [Espresso Coffee Guide Mocha Java](https://espressocoffeeguide.com/gourmet-coffee/asian-indonesian-and-pacific-coffees/mocha-java-coffee) **[unverified page-level]**

## Q4. Processing-method flavor effects (washed, natural, honey, anaerobic/carbonic maceration, wet-hulled) as applied in these origins

### Takeaway
Across sources: washed = clean, bright, floral/citrus, lighter body; natural = fruit-forward (berry, wine), heavier body, higher sweetness, more variance; honey sits between; anaerobic/carbonic = intense, "funky"/winey, sometimes overwhelming origin character. Origin pairings: Ethiopia, Burundi, Rwanda (Gakenke) and Uganda (Rwenzori) all produce both washed and natural; Kenya and Mt Elgon are almost entirely washed; Yemen and Harrar are traditionally natural. Wet-hulled (Giling Basah) is an Indonesian method and was not found in any African/Arabian origin source.

### Cited Findings
- Washed: "cleaner flavour profile with great clarity... emphasizes acidity with a low-to-medium body" — [Origin Coffee: What's coffee processing](https://origincoffee.co.uk/blogs/journal/whats-coffee-processing) (via search snippet, **[unverified page-level]**)
- Natural: cherries dried with fruit and mucilage intact, "very bright, vibrant and intense cup, often with high sweetness and a heavy body" — [Press Coffee](https://presscoffee.com/pages/washed-vs-natural-process-coffee), [Zest Coffee](https://www.zestcoffee.com.au/blogs/news/a-guide-to-coffee-processing-what-it-is-explained) (snippet-level)
- Honey: between washed and natural; "balances washed-coffee clarity with natural-coffee fruit"; the term refers to mucilage stickiness during drying, not flavor — [Coutume Café](https://coutumecafe.com/en/blogs/blog-cafe/les-m-thodes-de-fermentation-du-caf), [CoffeeGeek: impact of processing](https://coffeegeek.com/blog/farming/the-impact-of-processing-on-coffees-character/) (snippet-level)
- Carbonic maceration: whole cherries in a CO2-filled tank (wine technique); described as fruity and light-bodied by one source, "funky and complex" by another; some roasters find it "overwhelms the underlying bean character" — snippet synthesis of the same set; caveat: a claim that anaerobic process signatures are "three times stronger than washed coffees, citing a survey of 12,500 coffees" appears on one retail blog only and is unverified — [How coffee processing affects taste (retail blog)](https://d9143noja0cpe.cloudfront.net/blog/how-coffee-processing-affects-taste-a-flavor-guide) **[unverified]**
- Ethiopia natural: Coffee Review describes natural Yirgacheffe as "more pungent than bright though still extravagant in its floral-toned aromatics" — [Coffee Review: natural Yirgacheffe Wote Konga](https://www.coffeereview.com/review/ethiopia-natural-yirgacheffe-wote-konga/)
- Harrar vs Yemen (both dry-processed): "winy acidity," Yemen has more body and richness and "Red-Sea wildness" — [Coffee Review: Mocha-Java blends](https://www.coffeereview.com/mocha-java-blends/)
- Uganda: DRUGAR = ground-dried unsorted cherry (low grade); washed Mt Elgon lots hand-pulped — [Sucafina Uganda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/uganda)
- Tanzania: fully washed is water-intensive and water scarcity in the south pushes natural processing development — [Sucafina Tanzania](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/tanzania)
- Rwanda: improved CWS processing added at least 40% export premium; older home pulping produced low-quality semi-washed coffee — [Sucafina Rwanda](https://www.sucafina.com/na/origins/rwanda)

### Inferences
Suggested `processing_flavor_effect` rows, **inferred** by combining the above; use as defaults, not source claims:
- Ethiopia washed: jasmine, bergamot, lemon, black tea; low body. Ethiopia natural: blueberry, strawberry, wine; heavier body, higher sweetness.
- Kenya washed: bright, high acidity (no origin-specific processing alternative found).
- Burundi/Rwanda washed: sweet, floral, clean; naturals and honeys are newer and fruitier (inferred from availability of those lots on Sucafina).
- Yemen/Harrar natural: winey, spicy, heavier body, "wild" fermentation edge.
- Anaerobic/carbonic: no African/Arabian origin-specific sourced examples; treat as an optional modifier with "intense, winey, funky" default.

### Gaps
- No source found describing wet-hulled processing in any African/Arabian origin; no source that quantifies cup outcomes from controlled trials (SCA/CQI scores by process).
- No Perfect Daily Grind or Sprudge page was retrieved (search returned none for this query).
- No origin-specific anaerobic/carbonic examples in Africa found (e.g., Ethiopian or Rwandan anaerobic lots not retrieved).
